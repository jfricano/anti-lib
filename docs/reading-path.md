# A Path Through the Anti-Library

You won't get through 1,033 books, and you don't need to. The plan is to keep pulling titles off the horizon at a steady pace, follow the threads that pull you, and let the rest stay as the edge of what you know.

## The rhythm

- **Two digests a week**, about 30 minutes each. That works out to roughly 100 books a year, or about a tenth of the list.
- **One track at a time.** Each track has 8 to 10 books that talk to each other, so each digest builds on the last one.
- **Alternate fiction and ideas** inside a track, so a novel is followed by the philosophy or science it raises.
- **One "go deeper" a month.** Pick the digest that hit hardest and read it in full, or read the chapter the digest recommends.
- **Mark progress** in the Status column of `anti-library.xlsx` (Digested, Reading, Read in full).

## The first ten, in order

These ten move outward from Slaughterhouse-Five, open the first three tracks, and use books you already own early on.

1. *The Sirens of Titan* by Vonnegut: free will and the Tralfamadorians again, from the other side
2. *The Order of Time* by Rovelli: the physics of being unstuck in time
3. *The Stranger* by Camus (on your shelf): the absurd hero
4. *The Myth of Sisyphus* by Camus: the philosophy behind it
5. *Prometheus Rising* by Robert Anton Wilson (on your shelf): reality tunnels as a user manual
6. *Catch-22* by Heller: Vonnegut's war twin
7. *Don't Sleep, There Are Snakes* by Everett (on your shelf): a language without recursion and a lost faith
8. *Stories of Your Life and Others* by Ted Chiang: language rewires how you experience time
9. *The Brothers Karamazov* by Dostoevsky: the first big canon book, with the Grand Inquisitor
10. *Wholeness and the Implicate Order* by Bohm (on your shelf): physics as an undivided whole
