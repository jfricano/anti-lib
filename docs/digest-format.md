# Proposed format for a book digest

One file per book, so each one pulls a title out of the anti-library. The goal is a 20 to 30 minute read that gives you the substance and also a feel for the writing itself, which is the part summaries usually lose.

## 1. The card (top of page)
- Title, author, year, length, and how long it would take to read in full
- **Why it's on your list:** one line tying it to your shelf (for example, "Pynchon is what happens when Illuminatus! gets a physics degree")
- **Vonnegut meter:** how close it sits to Slaughterhouse-Five in feel, with one sentence on why

## 2. The story or argument (cliff notes)
- For fiction: the plot in 5 to 8 movements, then the main characters, each in a line
- For nonfiction and philosophy: the core claim, the 3 to 5 load-bearing arguments, and the strongest objection
- Spoilers are on, since the aim is to know the book

## 3. How the writing works (the part a digest normally drops)
This section carries the most weight. It has 4 to 6 short passages quoted from the book. Each one comes with:
- **What it's doing:** the technique at work (free indirect style, a recurring motif, the rhythm of the sentences, an unreliable narrator, an image system, the comedy of a deadpan line)
- **Why it lands:** how that technique makes you feel or see something a plain summary can't
- **Echoes:** where the same move shows up in books you love (for example, Vonnegut's "So it goes" next to Beckett's refrains)

## 4. What the experts say
- 3 to 4 critics or scholars, each with a short paraphrase or quote and attribution (for example, Nabokov on Gogol, Bloom on Shakespeare, James Wood on free indirect style)
- One point where the critics disagree, so you get the live debate

## 5. Ideas you keep
- 5 to 7 takeaways or questions worth carrying around
- The one line from the book worth remembering

## 6. Connections and next step
- 2 to 3 links to other titles in your anti-library (read next, read against, or the book it argues with)
- **Worth reading in full?** A verdict (yes, sample it, or the digest is enough), and if the answer is sample, which chapter

Quoted passages stay short, enough to show the style, never whole chapters.
