# Proposed Cuts: One Critical Pass

*Applied 2026-10-11: tiers A and B are cut (141 titles; Something Wicked This Way Comes and Point Counter Point were spared because jason likes the Bradbury/Huxley crew). Tier C was then cut too, except the "canon on every list" group, which became its own section, The Other Canon (Foundation stayed in sci-fi). The list is now 1,117 titles.*

*2026-10-11. Original proposal follows. Say "cut them all," name the tiers (A, B, C), or strike individual titles and I'll apply the rest.*

## The short answer

Yes, the net went too wide, but not where you'd expect. The new additions mostly earned their place. The bloat is older: completism (seven Platos, seven Pynchons, four Greenes, five McKennas), generic canon that connects to nothing on your shelf, and pop nonfiction that a better book already covers.

Cutting all 259 proposals takes the list from **1340 to 1081**, back to about where it was before this round of additions. Every section survives. The smallest after cuts are the new Writing About Television, at 7, and the Charter, at 9.

**The rule I used is one door per author.** For each writer, keep the entry point and the book closest to your taste. If a digest hooks you, the rest of their shelf reveals itself, which is the anti-library working as Eco meant it. Seneca would add that a list you can actually see the edges of is one you'll actually use.

**Never cut:** anything you own, the 12-book core shelf, anything marked Formative or Reading.

**Kept on purpose:** all of Vonnegut's own books (he's the center of gravity), the authors you named outright (Dickens, Twain, King, Dostoevsky, Bukowski, Ginsberg, Pound, Waits, Yorke, Jack White), and the respectful-fringe books, so the consciousness shelf keeps both poles.
