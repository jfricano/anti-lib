# Movies: Proposed Additions, Pass One

*Status: everything here was merged into movies.md on 2026-10-11 (1,290 films). Little Big Man, Room 237 and A Glitch in the Matrix were already on the list, and the Blue Velvet note was folded into its existing entry.*

*One read through the 1,093-film list, plus the films the music pass surfaced, looking for patterns that emerged between the selections and the holes they point at. Nothing here is on the list yet. Say "add them all," or name the clusters you want, and I'll merge them into the shelves.*

Two kinds of notes below. **What emerged** says what pattern I saw in the list you already have. **Other lists** flags things the book or music lists missed that this pass surfaced.

---

## 1. The hidden connectors

Three people turned out to link your favorites in ways the list didn't say out loud.

**Terry Southern** co-wrote Dr. Strangelove *and* Easy Rider, the two poles of the counterculture shelf. He is the missing link between "gallows satire" and "psychedelic road movie."
- [x] **The Loved One** (1965) · Tony Richardson. Southern's script from Evelyn Waugh's Hollywood-funeral satire. Death as a business. Six Feet Under's great-grandfather.
- [x] **The Magic Christian** (1969) · Joseph McGrath. Peter Sellers and Ringo Starr pay people to debase themselves. From Southern's novel.
- [x] **Barbarella** (1968) · Roger Vadim. Southern co-wrote. Camp sci-fi, and the source of Duran Duran's name, for the music list.

**Yuen Woo-ping** choreographed The Matrix *and* Kill Bill. One person's hands are behind two of your favorites.
- [x] **Iron Monkey** (1993) · Yuen Woo-ping. His own best film as director.
- [x] **Fist of Legend** (1994) · Gordon Chan. Jet Li; Yuen's choreography. The fight The Matrix's dojo scene is quoting.
- [x] **Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon** (2000) · Ang Lee. Yuen again, and the bamboo-forest fight.
- [x] **Drunken Master** (1978) · Yuen Woo-ping. Young Jackie Chan.

**Shane Black** wrote Lethal Weapon and The Last Boy Scout (already listed), and the Die Hard with a Vengeance formula is his. One more is missing:
- [x] **The Long Kiss Goodnight** (1996) · Renny Harlin. Samuel L. Jackson as a cut-rate PI and Geena Davis as an amnesiac assassin. The most Die Hard 3 film not named Die Hard 3.

## 2. The Slaughterhouse-Five family tree

*What emerged:* the Vonnegut shelf is built on tone, but there's a literal lineage too.

- [x] **The World According to Garp** (1982) · George Roy Hill. Same director as Slaughterhouse-Five, and John Irving was Vonnegut's student at Iowa. Irving is already on the book list; this closes the loop.
- [x] **Slap Shot** (1977) · George Roy Hill. Profane, funny, violent hockey. The Hill film closest to your wheelhouse.
- [x] **Back to School** (1986) · Alan Metter. Rodney Dangerfield hires Kurt Vonnegut (playing himself) to write his Vonnegut paper. Gets a D.
- [x] **A Ghost Story** (2017) · David Lowery. A sheeted ghost watches centuries pass through one house. The most unstuck-in-time film since Slaughterhouse-Five.
- [x] **Peppermint Candy** (2000) · Lee Chang-dong. A man's life told backward through twenty years of Korean history. Lee is already on the list twice; this is his Billy Pilgrim film.
- [x] **Little Big Man** (1970) · Arthur Penn. A 121-year-old narrator who was at Little Bighorn. Picaresque, funny, and sad about massacres.
- [x] **The Razor's Edge** (1984) · John Byrum. Bill Murray's passion project: a WWI vet goes looking for meaning in India. He made Ghostbusters to get this financed. *Other lists:* Maugham isn't on the book list.
- [x] **Stranger Than Fiction** (2006) · Marc Forster. Will Ferrell hears a novelist narrating his life and learns he's scheduled to die. Metafiction with a heart. Your Other Guys star in a Vonnegut plot.
- [x] **Dark Star** (1974) · John Carpenter. Astronauts argue phenomenology with a bomb that wants to detonate. Absurdist space comedy, made for nothing.
- [x] **Miracle Mile** (1988) · Steve De Jarnatt. A man answers a payphone in LA and learns the missiles land in seventy minutes. Cat's Cradle as a date movie.
- [x] **The Atomic Cafe** (1982) · Loader, Rafferty & Rafferty. A collage of 1950s civil-defense films. Gallows humor with no narrator needed.
- [x] **When the Wind Blows** (1986) · Jimmy Murakami. An animated elderly couple follows government pamphlets through nuclear winter. Raymond Briggs.
- [x] **Palm Springs** (2020) · Max Barbakow. A time loop at a wedding. The funniest recent descendant of Groundhog Day.
- [x] **Timecrimes** (2007) · Nacho Vigalondo. A small, mean, perfect time-loop thriller.

## 3. Charlie Kaufman and the blocked-writer shelf

*What emerged:* 8½, Barton Fink, Adaptation, Wonder Boys, Synecdoche, Naked Lunch, Misery, The Shining. That's a whole shelf about the writer who can't write. Two gaps:
- [x] **Confessions of a Dangerous Mind** (2002) · George Clooney. Kaufman's script. Chuck Barris, host of The Gong Show, claims he was also a CIA assassin. Tongue-in-cheek and stylized to the hilt.
- [x] **Human Nature** (2001) · Michel Gondry. Kaufman's first produced script, about a man raised as an ape. Flawed, but it completes the set.
- [x] **Providence** (1977) · Alain Resnais. A dying novelist rewrites his family in his head all night. Resnais is already on the list three times.
- [x] **Deconstructing Harry** (1997) · Woody Allen. A novelist's characters confront him. Allen's Wild Strawberries.
- [x] **Living in Oblivion** (1995) · Tom DiCillo. Steve Buscemi tries to direct an indie film. Everything goes wrong, three times.

- [x] **Ishtar** (1987) · Elaine May. The famous flop. Funnier than its reputation.

## 6. Hitchcock, Nietzsche and Dostoevsky

*What emerged:* the philosophy shelf has Nietzsche in Turin and Pascal's wager, but not the thriller that stages the Übermensch.
- [x] **Rope** (1948) · Alfred Hitchcock. Two students murder a friend to prove they're superior men, then host a dinner over the body. One continuous take.
- [x] **Compulsion** (1959) · Richard Fleischer. The same Leopold-Loeb case; Orson Welles's closing speech.
- [x] **Match Point** (2005) · Woody Allen. Crime and Punishment with a tennis pro, and luck as the only god.
- [x] **The Man Who Wasn't There** (2001) · Joel & Ethan Coen. A barber commits a crime and feels nothing. Camus's The Stranger as 1949 noir, with a courtroom lecture on Heisenberg's uncertainty principle. Links three shelves at once.
- [x] **The Hudsucker Proxy** (1994) · Joel & Ethan Coen. Capra by way of Kafka. The Coens' most underrated; opens your golden era.
- [x] **Le Feu Follet** (1963) · Louis Malle. A man's last day before his planned suicide, walking through Paris saying goodbye. Camus's question asked quietly.
- [x] **Le Trou** (1960) · Jacques Becker. A prison escape in near silence. Bresson's A Man Escaped is already in; this is its twin.

## 7. The Matrix, further back

*What emerged:* the simulation cluster (Dark City, Thirteenth Floor, eXistenZ) skipped the original.
- [x] **World on a Wire** (1973) · Rainer Werner Fassbinder. A simulated city whose inhabitants don't know it, decades before The Matrix. From Daniel Galouye's Simulacron-3, the same novel as The Thirteenth Floor. *Other lists:* the novel isn't on the book list.
- [x] **Pleasantville** (1998) · Gary Ross. Two 90s kids trapped in a black-and-white sitcom, and color spreads as people wake up. Plato's cave with a laugh track.
- [x] **The Truman Show** is in; its paranoid cousin isn't: **The Game** (1997) · David Fincher. Michael Douglas's life gets rewritten by a company he paid.
- [x] **Brainstorm** (1983) · Douglas Trumbull. A machine records experience, then someone records their own death. Natalie Wood's last film.
- [x] **Colossus: The Forbin Project** (1970) · Joseph Sargent. Two superintelligences find each other and take over. The dry original of every AI film.
- [x] **WarGames** (1983) · John Badham. "The only winning move is not to play." Game theory, nuclear war, a teenager.
- [x] **Computer Chess** (2013) · Andrew Bujalski. A 1980 chess-program tournament, shot on period video, slides into metaphysics. Made for the Hofstadter shelf.
- [x] **AlphaGo** (2017) · Greg Kohs. Lee Sedol versus the machine, and move 37. More moving than it has any right to be.
- [x] **Unbreakable** (2000) · M. Night Shyamalan. Bruce Willis as a superhero who doesn't know it. The quietest golden-era blockbuster.

## 8. Consciousness from the inside

*What emerged:* the book list has twenty-five titles on consciousness, but the film list treats it mostly through sci-fi. These are first-person accounts.
- [x] **The Diving Bell and the Butterfly** (2007) · Julian Schnabel. Locked-in syndrome, shot from inside one blinking eye. *Other lists:* Bauby's memoir belongs on the book list.
- [x] **Awakenings** (1990) · Penny Marshall. De Niro and Robin Williams, from Oliver Sacks's book. Sacks is on the book list; this is the only film of his work.
- [x] **The Father** (2020) · Florian Zeller. Dementia from inside the head. The apartment rearranges itself.
- [x] **Marjorie Prime** (2017) · Michael Almereyda. An old woman talks to a hologram of her dead husband. Quiet and devastating.
- [x] **Fast, Cheap & Out of Control** (1997) · Errol Morris. A lion tamer, a topiary gardener, a mole-rat expert and a robot scientist. About what minds are. Morris's best.
- [x] **Vernon, Florida** (1981) · Errol Morris. Townspeople philosophize about turkeys and turtles.
- [x] **Copenhagen** (2002) · Howard Davies. Michael Frayn's play: Heisenberg visits Bohr in 1941. Heisenberg's Physics and Philosophy is on the book list.
- [x] **Infinity** (1996) · Matthew Broderick. Feynman's first marriage, during the Manhattan Project. Small and sincere.
- [x] **Sunshine** (2007) · Danny Boyle. A crew flies a bomb into the dying sun. Alex Garland's script before Ex Machina.
- [x] **Mindwalk** (1990) · Bernt Capra. Already seen, so mark it [x], but it belongs on the shelf. *Other lists:* its source, The Tao of Physics, is already on the book list.

## 19. Smaller things I noticed

- [x] **Death Becomes Her** (1992) · Robert Zemeckis. Immortality as a curse, played for slapstick. Vonnegut's "Fortitude" as a Meryl Streep comedy.
- [x] **Dead Ringers** (1988) · David Cronenberg. Twin gynecologists. The Double Life of Véronique and The Prestige are in; this is the darkest double.
- [x] **Enemy** (2013) · Denis Villeneuve. Jake Gyllenhaal finds his double. From Saramago's The Double. *Other lists:* Saramago (Blindness, The Double) isn't on the book list.
- [x] **Why Has Bodhi-Dharma Left for the East?** (1989) · Bae Yong-kyun. A Zen koan as a film. For the Watts shelf.
- [x] **Enlightenment Guaranteed** (2000) · Doris Dörrie. Two German brothers lose everything in Tokyo and end up at a Zen monastery. Funny.
- [x] **Walkabout** is in. Add **Wake in Fright** (1971) · Ted Kotcheff. A teacher stranded in an outback town drinks himself into hell. Lost for decades.
- [x] **The Devils** (1971) · Ken Russell. Nuns, possession, Oliver Reed burned at the stake. Still censored.
- [x] **Sorcerer** is in. Add **The Exorcist III** (1990) · William Peter Blatty. One jump scare people still talk about, and a long theological argument.
- [x] **Kiss of the Spider Woman** (1985) · Héctor Babenco. Two cellmates, one tells movies. Manuel Puig's novel.
- [x] **Jacob's Ladder** is in. Add **Angel Heart** (1987) · Alan Parker. Mickey Rourke, New Orleans, and a client named Louis Cyphre.
- [x] **The Hunger** (1983) · Tony Scott. Bowie as an aging vampire; Bauhaus plays the opening. Music-list crossover.
- [x] **Blue Velvet** carries an Ebert one-star warning on the list. Worth knowing David Foster Wallace called it the film that made him want to write; his Lynch essay is in A Supposedly Fun Thing on the book list. Keep the Ebert note, add the DFW one.
