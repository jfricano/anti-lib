# Movies: Proposed Cuts, Final Critical Pass

*A critical read of all 1,298 films. Verdict: the net isn't too wide, but it caught filler. 76 cuts are proposed below (about 6%), leaving about 1,222. **Decision (2026-10-11):** only section 3 was approved; 12 of its films were removed (Tenet, Scott Pilgrim and Olivier's Hamlet were kept), leaving 1,286. Sections 1 and 2 stay on the list, and the structural suggestion in section 5 was dropped.*

**What I checked.** Every entry was asked three questions. Is there something to discover here, or is it a film everyone has already seen or will stumble into? Does it connect to the books, the music, or the stated taste (stylized, funny, sometimes violent, ideas-heavy)? Does another film on the list already do the same job better?

**What I'm not cutting.** Scorsese (26 films) and Herzog (25 before these cuts) look heavy, but they're named favorites, so the depth is the point. The art-cinema shelves stay whole, because Tarkovsky being on the list says slow cinema is welcome. Weak adaptations of books on the reading list mostly stay too, since pairing a film with its book is the list's main job.

## 5. Structural suggestion: dissolve the two seed shelves

The *90s Volume* and *Ebert's Four-Star Shelf* were built from the books' spines, not their contents. After these cuts they hold about 40 films, mostly cross-references. Their surviving films could move to the shelves they actually belong on (Babe to Animation, Quiz Show to America, and so on), and the two books would be credited in the intro instead. That's optional. Say "dissolve" if you want it.

*The Cable Guy was dropped from the cuts, because the TV list's own pass makes the case for it as "a man raised by television." Eight TV-parody and media-satire films from that pass were added at the same time (Real Life, A Face in the Crowd, Broadcast News, Borat and four more).*
