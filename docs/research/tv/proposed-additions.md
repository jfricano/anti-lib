# Proposed Additions: TV, One Pass for Connections

*A reread of the 498-show TV list against the core shows and the other three lists, looking for the threads that run between them and what fell through. Nothing here has been added yet. Say "add them all," or name the ones you want, or the tiers.*

**Tiers:** ★★★ how did we miss this · ★★ strong fit · ★ worth a look

---

## Part 1: What emerged (the threads, and what we under-served)

**1. Your core is a canon of endings.**
Newhart's last scene, Six Feet Under's finale, the Sopranos cut to black, Seinfeld's trial, Breaking Bad and Saul landing their planes. Five of the most argued-over endings in TV history are on your "seen" shelf, and St. Elsewhere's snow globe is already on the list. That isn't an accident: you like shows that commit to an end. The list under-served this. It needs Roseanne (whose final season reveals the whole show was Roseanne's rewrite of her own life), Dinosaurs (a TGIF show that ends with an ice age killing the family), Quantum Leap ("Sam never returned home"), and Blackadder Goes Forth, which you've already seen and which is the model.

**2. Seinfeld's missing parent.**
Larry David and Michael Richards met on *Fridays* (ABC, 1980–82), where Andy Kaufman staged his famous on-air fight. Garry Shandling's *It's Garry Shandling's Show* (1986–90) broke the fourth wall and had no plot five years before Seinfeld, and Shandling's next show, Larry Sanders, is already shelf 1. Neither Fridays nor Garry Shandling's Show is on the list, and together with Kaufman's specials they're the room Seinfeld and Curb came out of.

**3. Nathan Fielder's family tree is longer than the cringe shelf shows.**
Fielder started on *This Hour Has 22 Minutes* (Canada). Before Ali G there was Louis Theroux's *Weird Weekends* (1998), the gentle-faced interviewer among cults and survivalists, and before that Michael Moore's *TV Nation* (1994), which pranked corporations; Theroux worked on it. Before all of them: *Candid Camera* (1948), Allen Funt's real people in staged situations. The Rehearsal's own great-grandparent is *An American Family* (on the list), which Albert Brooks parodied in *Real Life* (1979), which isn't on the film list and should be.

**4. The I Think You Should Leave aesthetic has a Chris Elliott spine.**
Letterman's Chris Elliott bits → *Get a Life* → *Eagleheart* (Adult Swim, 2011, Elliott as a Chuck Norris marshal) is one line. Tim Robinson's own prehistory is *The Characters* (Netflix, 2016, one episode per comedian; his is the ITYSL pilot in all but name). The list has the first two points on the line and neither of the last two.

**5. You have both poles of therapy on the shelf and didn't notice.**
Bob Newhart played a psychologist (The Bob Newhart Show, shelf 1); Tony Soprano sat across from Dr. Melfi. In Treatment and BeTipul are on the list; Dr. Katz too. Missing: *Couples Therapy* (Showtime, real sessions with Orna Guralnik, the documentary In Treatment), *En thérapie* (the French In Treatment, set after the Bataclan attacks) and *Shrinking*. Also *Frasier*, already listed, is a radio shrink; the thread runs through four shelves.

**6. The fourth wall is a lineage, and Zack Morris is in it.**
Burns and Allen talked to the camera in 1950. Dobie Gillis did it in 1959. Zack Morris's "time out" did it in 1989, Clarissa in 1991, Parker Lewis in 1990, Malcolm in 2000, Bernie Mac in 2001, Fleabag in 2016. Saved by the Bell, which you called trashy, is in a straight line to Fleabag. Parker Lewis, Clarissa and The Bernie Mac Show are missing.

**7. Vonnegut was on television, and the list didn't look.**
*Between Time and Timbuktu* (PBS, 1972) is a TV film mashing up his books, with Bob and Ray in it (the book pass just flagged Bob and Ray). *Who Am I This Time?* (1982) is Jonathan Demme directing Christopher Walken and Susan Sarandon in a Vonnegut story. *Kurt Vonnegut's Monkey House* (Showtime, 1991) is an anthology he hosted. *Harrison Bergeron* (1995) is a Showtime film with Christopher Plummer. None are on the film list either; they belong here as the Vonnegut shelf TV never had.

**8. The Michigan cluster.**
Detroiters (Detroit), Freaks and Geeks (Chippewa, Michigan), Tim Robinson and Sam Richardson (Detroit), Keegan-Michael Key (Detroit), Jack White (music list, Detroit), Paul Feig (Mount Clemens). *God, the Devil and Bob* (NBC, 2000) is about God and the Devil betting on a Detroit auto worker's soul, cancelled after four episodes, and it's pure Vonnegut. *Home Improvement* is Detroit too, TGIF, and you've probably seen it.

**9. Comedy as philosophy, the TV version.**
The book pass found Carlin and Karl Pilkington on your shelf and proposed a "Comedy as Philosophy" section. On TV that's: Carlin's fourteen HBO specials, *An Idiot Abroad* (Pilkington himself, sent around the world by Gervais and Merchant), *The Ricky Gervais Show* (the animated podcast where Pilkington became Pilkington), *The Richard Pryor Show* (NBC, 1977, four episodes before the network gave up), Steven Wright's 1985 HBO special (the root of deadpan one-liners), Norm Macdonald, and Stewart Lee's *Comedy Vehicle*, which takes jokes apart on stage the way Carlin did. The list had Chappelle and nobody else from stand-up.

**10. Music TV, and the music list, are talking to each other.**
Tom Waits sang "The Piano Has Been Drinking" on *Fernwood 2 Night* in 1977 (already shelf 1). *Night Music* (NBC, 1988–90, Hal Willner booking) put Sun Ra, Sonny Rollins and Leonard Cohen on the same stage. *Austin City Limits* has the 1978 Waits episode. *Classic Albums* and *Soundbreaking* are album-by-album TV. Radiohead did a South Park episode; Jack White did a Simpsons. The TV list had Treme, Flight of the Conchords and Get Back and nothing else musical.

**11. The unfinished shelf is a taste, not a category.**
Carnivàle, John from Cincinnati, Lodge 49, Patriot, Terriers, Freaks and Geeks, Get a Life, Wonderfalls, Rubicon, Police Squad!, Andy Richter Controls the Universe, My So-Called Life, Perpetual Grace LTD, Profit. The list has the first seven. Everything after is missing, and the pattern (one strange season, cancelled) is as much your taste as any genre.

**12. The Nick at Nite you watched was curated by someone with taste.**
Nick at Nite's 1985–95 lineup included SCTV and Fernwood 2 Night (both on shelf 1; you may have already seen them), plus Donna Reed, My Three Sons, Make Room for Daddy, Mork & Mindy, Welcome Back Kotter, Happy Days, Laverne & Shirley, The Brady Bunch, The Patty Duke Show and The Wonder Years. Several of those are probably seen; say which. The Wonder Years is the big miss: narrated nostalgia with a Vonnegut-grade "so it goes" wistfulness, and it ran exactly when you were the age of its narrator.

**13. Kids' TV built the taste too, and we skipped it.**
The brief said a TGIF shelf at most, so the list stopped there. But the kids' shows of your era carry the same DNA: *The Adventures of Pete & Pete* (indie-rock cameos, melancholy suburbia), *You Can't Do That on Television* (Canadian anarchic sketch), *Mr. Wizard's World*, *Square One TV* (whose "Mathnet" is a Dragnet parody; Dragnet is on the list), *Wishbone* (a dog acts out classic literature; the anti-library for kids), *The Muppet Show* (Henson's vaudeville backstage show with Alice Cooper and Peter Sellers; Dr. Teeth is Dr. John), *Fraggle Rock*, Henson's *The Storyteller*, and *Mister Rogers' Neighborhood*, which Kidding (on the list) and Joe Pera both descend from. Proposed as a small shelf of its own.

**14. Pynchon and Robert Anton Wilson on TV, gathered.**
TV almost never does Pynchon. The handful that do: Lodge 49, Rubicon (AMC, 2010, a paranoid conspiracy that ends in a crossword), Dispatches from Elsewhere (an alternate-reality game in Philadelphia, exactly The Crying of Lot 49), Gravity Falls (a kids' show built out of cryptograms and conspiracies), Welcome to the N.H.K. (a shut-in convinced a conspiracy made him one), 30 Coins, Wild Palms, Utopia. Four of those eight are missing.

**15. Three of your core shows are about making television.**
Dick Van Dyke writes a variety show. Newhart's Dick Loudon hosts a local TV show. Nathan and The Rehearsal are about staging. Larry Sanders, The Comeback, 30 Rock, Hacks and Boris are on the list; *UnREAL* (behind a Bachelor-style show, manipulation as craft), *The Other Two*, Ken Finkleman's *The Newsroom* (CBC, 1996, Larry Sanders in a Canadian newsroom) and *The Muppet Show* (a backstage show) are not.

**16. Small flags.**
Blackadder is now marked seen. Mork & Mindy, Happy Days, The Brady Bunch, Home Improvement, South Park, Family Guy, Archer, Frasier, Friends and The Office (US) are all likely seen and some aren't on the list at all; tell me which to add as seen so the count is honest. The Africa shelf is one title because almost no African series are exported; three are proposed below but it will stay thin.

## Part 2: Proposed additions, by thread

### A. Seinfeld's and Curb's missing parents
- ★★★ **It's Garry Shandling's Show** (1986–1990) · Showtime/Fox. The fourth wall, no plot, the studio audience as characters. Seinfeld's direct ancestor; Larry Sanders came next.
- ★★ **Fridays** (1980–1982) · ABC. The SNL knockoff where Larry David and Michael Richards met, and Andy Kaufman started a fight on air. Hard to find; worth the hunt for the Kaufman episode.
- ★★ **Andy's Funhouse / The Andy Kaufman Special** (1979) · ABC. Kaufman's one special, with the Foreign Man, the wrestling and the gospel choir. Taxi is on shelf 1; this is the real Kaufman.
- ★★ **Family Tree** (2013) · HBO. Christopher Guest's one series, improvised like his films. Curb's method in a gentler key.
- ★ **Reno 911!** (2003–) · Comedy Central. Improvised Cops parody. The State's reunion.
- ★ **Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee** (2012–2019). Seinfeld talking to the people whose jokes built your shelf, including Carlin's heirs. You've likely seen some.

### B. The Fielder tree
- ★★★ **Louis Theroux's Weird Weekends** (1998–2000) · BBC. The polite stranger among cults, survivalists and porn sets. The direct ancestor of the Fielder persona.
- ★★ **TV Nation** (1994–1995) · NBC/Fox. Michael Moore pranks corporations; Theroux was a correspondent. Nathan for You's business angle starts here.
- ★★ **Candid Camera** (1948–2014) · CBS. Allen Funt's real people in staged situations. The original.
- ★★ **Check It Out! with Dr. Steve Brule** (2010–2017) · Adult Swim. John C. Reilly as a damaged public-access host. Tim and Eric's best spin-off.
- ★★ **Kenny vs. Spenny** (2002–2010) · CBC/Showcase. Two friends in humiliation contests; Trey Parker and Matt Stone produced it in the US. Nathan for You's cruelty with the fig leaf removed.
- ★★ **The Tom Green Show** (1994–2000) · MTV. Ottawa public access turned national; the missing link between Letterman's stunts and Jackass and Eric Andre.
- ★★ **Jerrod Carmichael Reality Show** (2024) · HBO. A confessional reality show as self-examination. The Rehearsal's cousin.
- ★ **This Hour Has 22 Minutes** (1993–) · CBC. Where Fielder started. Canadian news satire.
- ★ **Delocated** (2009–2013) · Adult Swim. A man in witness protection stars in a reality show wearing a ski mask. Jon Glaser.
- ★ **UnREAL** (2015–2018) · Lifetime. Producers manipulate a Bachelor-style show. Darker than it had any right to be.

### C. The Chris Elliott and Tim Robinson line
- ★★ **Eagleheart** (2011–2014) · Adult Swim. Chris Elliott as an invincible marshal. Get a Life, twenty years on.
- ★★ **The Characters** (2016) · Netflix. One half hour per comedian; Tim Robinson's episode is ITYSL before ITYSL.
- ★★ **Police Squad!** (1982) · ABC. Six episodes from the Airplane! team; the joke density of peak Simpsons in live action. Cancelled because, the network said, "you had to watch it."
- ★ **Three Busy Debras** (2020–2022) · Adult Swim. Three suburban women in a pastel nightmare. Robinson-adjacent.
- ★ **Stella** (2005) · Comedy Central. Showalter, Wain and Black in suits, surreal and polite.
- ★ **The Grinder** (2015–2016) · Fox. Rob Lowe as a TV lawyer who thinks he's a lawyer, with Fred Savage. One meta season.
- ★ **Trial & Error** (2017–2018) · NBC. True-crime mockumentary with John Lithgow.
- ★ **Documentary Now!** (2015–) · IFC. Armisen, Hader and Meyers parody one documentary per episode, including a Talking Heads concert film and an Eagles-style band. Pairs with the music list.

### F. Endings, and the shows that commit to them

## Part 4: Three format proposals that came out of the pass

1. **An `hours` column.** The TV wing is the only one where a pick costs a week. The Wire is 60 hours, Fawlty Towers is 6, Too Many Cooks is 11 minutes. A rough hours-to-finish per show lets the page sort "short first" and lets a digest say what a show costs before it says what it gives.

2. **A `watch` column: all / seasons / one season / episodes.** The notes already say "seasons 1–3 only" and "Part 8 is a work of art" in passing. Make it a field, so the anti-library's verdict on *how much* of a show is the show is visible, the way the book digests will say "read in full or not."

3. **Cross-medium neighbors.** The books' "shelf neighbor" line names a book. For TV, the richest neighbors are often across lists: Tales from the Tour Bus → the music list, The Critic → the Ebert shelf, Catch-22 → the novel, Wishbone → the books. Let `neighbor` point at any list, and let the page link across tabs.
