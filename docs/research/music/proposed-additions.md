## 1. Vonnegut actually has a discography

**What emerged:** the lists treat Vonnegut as a sensibility, but there are records with his name or his words on them, and a whole cluster of twentieth-century music written in the shadow of the same firebombings.

- [ ] ★ Dave Soldier & Kurt Vonnegut — *Ice-9 Ballads* (2001). Vonnegut reads texts from *Cat's Cradle* over Soldier's chamber-jazz settings. The only record where you hear the Bokononist calypsos in his own voice.
- [ ] Ambrosia — *Ambrosia* (1975). "Nice, Nice, Very Nice" sets the Calypso from *Cat's Cradle*; Vonnegut has a co-writing credit and liked it.
- [ ] Al Stewart — *Modern Times* (1975). Has a song called "Sirens of Titan," written from the novel.
- [ ] ★ Richard Strauss — *Metamorphosen* (1945). Written for 23 solo strings in the weeks after the bombing of Dresden and Munich, by an 80-year-old watching his world burn. The closest thing to *Slaughterhouse-Five* in classical music.
- [ ] George Crumb — *Black Angels* (1970; Kronos Quartet, 1990). An electric string quartet "in time of war," written during Vietnam. Dated "Friday the 13th, March 1970 (in tempore belli)."
- [ ] Jimi Hendrix — *Band of Gypsys* (1970). "Machine Gun" is Vietnam as twelve minutes of guitar; the most direct musical statement on the war.
- [ ] The Dresden Dolls — *The Dresden Dolls* (2003). "Brechtian punk cabaret" from Boston, named partly for the bombing; Amanda Palmer sings like Weill with a hangover.
- [ ] Note: Shostakovich's *String Quartet No. 8* (already on the list) was written in Dresden in 1960 while he was scoring a film about the firebombing. Worth adding that to its note.

## 2. The book list has records in it

**What emerged:** half the poets and novelists you listed either made records, wrote for them, or had composers set them. The music list caught the obvious ones (Kerouac, Ginsberg readings, Bukowski's *Hostage*) and missed these.

- [ ] ★ Ezra Pound — *Le Testament de Villon* (1923; recorded 1972). Pound wrote an opera. It sets François Villon's medieval poems in his own strange, drumming idiom. Nobody expects it.
- [ ] Philip Glass & Allen Ginsberg — *Hydrogen Jukebox* (1990). A chamber opera built from Ginsberg's poems, from "Howl" to "Wichita Vortex Sutra." Both men perform on it.
- [ ] Allen Ginsberg — *The Ballad of the Skeletons* (1996). His last single, with Paul McCartney on guitar and Philip Glass on piano.
- [ ] *Jack Kerouac Reads On the Road* (1999). Lost Kerouac tapes, plus Tom Waits and Primus performing "On the Road" to a Kerouac lyric. Your two Waits threads meet here.
- [ ] Ken Kesey & the Merry Pranksters — *The Acid Test* (1966). The actual soundtrack of the Acid Tests, with the Grateful Dead in the room. Kesey is on the book list; this is his record.
- [ ] Material — *Seven Souls* (1989). William S. Burroughs reads from *The Western Lands* over Bill Laswell's Moroccan trance-funk. Burroughs sounds like a prophet here, not a novelty.
- [ ] Lotion — *Nobody's Cool* (1995). An indie band whose liner notes were written by Thomas Pynchon, the only ones he ever did for a rock record.
- [ ] Spike Jones — *Spiked!* (1994 compilation). 1940s musical slapstick, with liner notes by Pynchon. The lineage runs Jones → Zappa → the Residents.
- [ ] ★ Erik Satie — *Socrate* (1918). A "symphonic drama" setting Plato's dialogues, including the death of Socrates, in music so plain it's almost nothing. Plato is on the book list; this is him sung.
- [ ] Leonard Bernstein — *Serenade (after Plato's Symposium)* (1954). Each movement is one speaker from the *Symposium* on love.
- [ ] Claudio Monteverdi — *Il ritorno d'Ulisse in patria* (1640). The *Odyssey*'s homecoming as early opera; pairs with *O Brother* on the list.
- [ ] Franz Liszt — *Dante Symphony* (1857). Inferno and Purgatorio for orchestra.
- [ ] Alfred Schnittke — *Faust Cantata* (1983). The 1587 Faust chapbook set with a tango for the devil. Goethe and Mann's *Doctor Faustus* are both on the book list.
- [ ] Morton Feldman — *Neither* (1977). An "opera" on 87 words by Samuel Beckett, for one soprano and orchestra, an hour long. Beckett is on the book list.
- [ ] Luciano Berio — *Thema (Omaggio a Joyce)* (1958). Cathy Berberian reads the "Sirens" chapter of *Ulysses*; Berio dissolves her voice into electronic music. Joyce as sound.
- [ ] Roger Miller — *Big River* (1985 cast album). *Huckleberry Finn* as a country musical by the man who wrote "King of the Road." Lighter than everything above, and good.

## 3. Your counterculture shelf is a record collection

**What emerged:** Robert Anton Wilson, Leary, Watts and McKenna are fifteen books on your shelf and nearly nothing on the music list. The list only had the KLF and Tool. All of these men made or appear on records.

- [ ] ★ Alan Watts — *This Is It* (1962). Watts, Henry Jacobs and friends chanting, drumming and laughing in a studio; the sound of *The Book* before it was written. Watts is all over your shelf.
- [ ] Robert Anton Wilson & The Golden Horde — *The Chocolate Biscuit Conspiracy* (1984). RAW fronting an Irish garage band, reciting Illuminatus material over fuzz. It exists.
- [ ] Timothy Leary — *Turn On, Tune In, Drop Out* (1966). Leary's spoken guide to the trip, released as an LP the year Revolver's "Tomorrow Never Knows" quoted his *Psychedelic Experience*.
- [ ] Timothy Leary & Ash Ra Tempel — *Seven Up* (1973). Leary, on the run in Switzerland, sings with the German cosmic-rock band already on your list.
- [ ] The Shamen — *Boss Drum* (1992). "Re:Evolution" is Terence McKenna delivering his whole thesis over techno. It charted in Britain.
- [ ] Shpongle — *Are You Shpongled?* (1998). Psychedelic trance built on McKenna samples; the DMT shelf's house band.
- [ ] ★ *The Voyager Golden Record* (1977; reissued 2017). The record NASA sent out of the solar system: Blind Willie Johnson, Chuck Berry, Bach played by Glenn Gould, a Navajo night chant, Javanese gamelan. Carl Sagan's mixtape for aliens, and a Vonnegut artifact if there ever was one.

## 4. The "Losing My Edge" syllabus

**What emerged:** you named LCD Soundsystem in your top five. "Losing My Edge" is a list of about forty bands, and your music list already has most of them (Can, Suicide, Beefheart, Gil Scott-Heron, the Sonics, Scott Walker, Daft Punk, Pere Ubu, the Slits, Joy Division, PiL, the Monks, Sun Ra, Mahavishnu, Cabaret Voltaire, Eric B. & Rakim, Basic Channel, Pharoah Sanders, the Fall, Alice Coltrane, Steve Reich, the Stooges, the Birthday Party). These are the ones it doesn't have, plus the New York dance-punk lineage LCD came out of.

- [ ] ★ Manuel Göttsching — *E2-E4* (1984). One hour, one guitar, one sequencer, recorded in a single take. The Ash Ra Tempel guitarist invents house music by accident. Named in the song.
- [ ] Cybotron — *Enter* (1983). Juan Atkins, named in the song, inventing Detroit techno out of Kraftwerk and Parliament.
- [ ] Nation of Ulysses — *13-Point Program to Destroy America* (1991). DC punk as a fake political party. Named in the song.
- [ ] Heldon — *Electronique Guerilla* (1974). French proto-industrial guitar-and-synth, named in the song.
- [ ] ★ ESG — *Come Away with ESG* (1983). Three sisters from the South Bronx; bass, drums, congas, almost nothing else. Sampled by everyone; the root of the whole DFA sound.
- [ ] Liquid Liquid — *Optimo* (1983). "Cavern" became "White Lines." Four tracks, all rhythm.
- [ ] A Certain Ratio — *To Each...* (1981). Manchester post-punk discovering Latin percussion.
- [ ] Dinosaur L — *24→24 Music* (1981). Arthur Russell's disco project; "Go Bang!" is James Murphy's Rosetta stone.
- [ ] Larry Levan — *Live at the Paradise Garage* (2000 release). The DJ set that the song's narrator claims to have witnessed.
- [ ] The Rapture — *Echoes* (2003). The first DFA record that mattered; "House of Jealous Lovers."
- [ ] *DFA Compilation #2* (2004). The label's house style in one box: LCD, the Juan MacLean, Black Dice, Delia Gonzalez & Gavin Russom.
- [ ] Hercules & Love Affair — *Hercules & Love Affair* (2008). Disco revival with Antony singing "Blind."
- [ ] Cut Copy — *In Ghost Colours* (2008). The Australian answer to *Sound of Silver*.
- [ ] Paul McCartney — *McCartney II* (1980). A home-recorded synth oddity James Murphy and his peers rediscovered; "Temporary Secretary" is proto-LCD. Also one Beatle record you may not know.

## 5. Around *Illinois*

**What emerged:** you called *Illinois* the biggest bang for one album. Its sound is Steve Reich plus Vince Guaraldi plus a church choir, and its circle of collaborators made a cluster of records the list skipped.

- [ ] ★ Sufjan Stevens, Bryce Dessner, Nico Muhly & James McAlister — *Planetarium* (2017). A song cycle on the planets, with trombones and electronics. Your physics shelf, sung.
- [ ] Danielson — *Ships* (2006). Sufjan plays on it; Daniel Smith's family band is where Sufjan's brass-and-choir maximalism came from.
- [ ] Final Fantasy (Owen Pallett) — *He Poos Clouds* (2006). A violin-and-loops song cycle about the eight schools of Dungeons & Dragons magic. Pallett arranged strings for Arcade Fire.
- [ ] Nico Muhly — *Mothertongue* (2008). A song cycle built from lists of addresses and the sounds of language itself. Sits next to Everett on your linguistics shelf.
- [ ] Cassandra Jenkins — *An Overview on Phenomenal Nature* (2021). Seven songs about grief, a security guard who meditates, and the sea; sax and spoken word. The most *Illinois*-like record of this decade.
- [ ] The Books — *The Lemon of Pink* (2003). Found-sound collage with cello and banjo; funny and tender, built from thrift-store tapes.
- [ ] Jens Lekman — *Night Falls Over Kortedala* (2007). Swedish chamber pop with Sufjan's gift for a specific, named detail.

## 6. The Beatles, sideways

**What emerged:** you know all the Beatles, so the move is outward: the records that fed them, the records they fed, and the odd corners of the solo years.

- [ ] ★ Harry Nilsson — *Pandemonium Shadow Show* (1967). The record the Beatles called their favorite American album; it has a Beatles medley on it.
- [ ] Badfinger — *Straight Up* (1971). The Apple band; "Baby Blue," "Day After Day."
- [ ] Emitt Rhodes — *Emitt Rhodes* (1970). One man in a garage making a better McCartney record than McCartney made that year.
- [ ] Paul & Linda McCartney — *Ram* (1971). Dismissed at the time, now the one critics love; homemade, loose and odd.
- [ ] Yoko Ono — *Yoko Ono/Plastic Ono Band* (1970). The twin of John's record, recorded the same week; screamed free-jazz rock with Ornette Coleman on one track. More influential on the avant-garde than John's.
- [ ] Jellyfish — *Spilt Milk* (1993). Power pop that out-Beatles the Beatles in the studio.
- [ ] of Montreal — *Hissing Fauna, Are You the Destroyer?* (2007). *Sgt. Pepper's* run through a depressive episode in Norway.
- [ ] Carl Perkins — *Dance Album of Carl Perkins* (1957). George Harrison's whole guitar style is in here.
- [ ] The Shirelles — *Tonight's the Night* (1961). The girl group the early Beatles covered ("Boys," "Baby It's You").
- [ ] The Rutles — *The Rutles* (1978). Neil Innes's parody is so exact it got sued; the funniest thing ever done with the Beatles, and your Vonnegut sensibility will like it.

## 7. Your three loves, further out

**Tom Waits.** The list has Waits's sources (Beefheart, Partch, Weill, Howlin' Wolf). It missed the people who sound like his cousins and the records he made for film.
- [ ] ★ Mose Allison — *Mose Allison Sings* (1963 compilation). Jazz piano and a sly Mississippi drawl; the "Young Man Blues" writer. Waits's cool-era phrasing starts here.
- [ ] Screamin' Jay Hawkins — *At Home with Screamin' Jay Hawkins* (1958). Coffins, bones, "I Put a Spell on You." Jarmusch built *Mystery Train* around him.
- [ ] Tom Waits — *One from the Heart* (1982). The Coppola soundtrack, duets with Crystal Gayle; the last of the lounge Waits and strangely lovely.
- [ ] Tom Waits — *Night on Earth* (1992). His score for the Jarmusch film, instrumental and wheezing.
- [ ] Chuck E. Weiss — *Extremely Cool* (1999). The "Chuck E.'s in Love" guy, produced by Waits; Hollywood hustler blues.
- [ ] Joe Henry — *Scar* (2001). Noir jazz-folk with Ornette Coleman soloing on the opener. The best Waits record Waits didn't make.
- [ ] Beth Gibbons & Rustin Man — *Out of Season* (2002). Portishead's singer with Talk Talk's bassist, making a smoky 1960s folk-jazz record.
- [ ] Man Man — *Six Demon Bag* (2006). Philadelphia junkyard cabaret, Waits played at double speed by people in war paint.

**Jack White.** The list has Son House, Robert Johnson and the Stooges. It missed his actual Detroit ancestors and the rockabilly and surf he mines.
- [ ] ★ Flat Duo Jets — *Go Go Harlem Baby* (1991). Dex Romweber's two-piece is the band Jack White has called his biggest influence; the White Stripes' format comes from here.
- [ ] The Gories — *I Know You Fine, but How You Doin'* (1990). Detroit garage-blues, no bass, a decade before the Stripes.
- [ ] Hasil Adkins — *Out to Hunch* (1986 compilation). A one-man band from West Virginia who recorded in a shack. "No More Hot Dogs."
- [ ] Link Wray & His Ray Men — *Link Wray & the Wraymen* (1960). "Rumble" invented the power chord and got banned from radio as an instrumental.
- [ ] Dick Dale & His Del-Tones — *Surfers' Choice* (1962). Surf guitar as controlled violence; *Pulp Fiction* opens with him.
- [ ] Blind Willie McTell — *The Definitive Blind Willie McTell* (1927–35). Twelve-string Atlanta blues; the Stripes covered "Your Southern Can Is Mine," and Dylan wrote his greatest unreleased song about him.
- [ ] Bob Dylan — *The Bootleg Series, Vols. 1–3* (1991). The outtakes, including "Blind Willie McTell" and "Series of Dreams"; for someone who knows early Dylan, this is the hidden Dylan.
- [ ] Roy Orbison — *In Dreams* (1963). The operatic voice behind *Blue Velvet* and half of Jack White's ballads.
- [ ] Nancy Sinatra & Lee Hazlewood — *Nancy & Lee* (1968). Cowboy psychedelia; "Some Velvet Morning." Hazlewood is the missing link between Waits, Nick Cave and Tarantino's needle drops.
- [ ] Lee Hazlewood — *Cowboy in Sweden* (1970). His strange exile record. Nobody who likes Cohen or Cave has failed to fall for it.
- [ ] Brendan Benson — *Lapalco* (2002). White's Raconteurs partner, before the Raconteurs; Detroit power pop.

**Radiohead and Thom Yorke.** The list has Aphex, Autechre, Scott Walker, Can and Talk Talk. It missed the actual side records and the classical links.
- [ ] ★ Jonny Greenwood & Krzysztof Penderecki — *Threnody for the Victims of Hiroshima / Popcorn Superhet Receiver / Polymorphia / 48 Responses to Polymorphia* (2012). Greenwood's answers to the Polish composer who shaped *Kid A*'s strings, on one album with the originals.
- [ ] Jonny Greenwood — *Bodysong* (2003). His first film score; where the Penderecki and the Alice Coltrane start showing.
- [ ] Steve Reich — *Radio Rewrite* (2014). Reich rewrites two Radiohead songs, and Greenwood plays *Electric Counterpoint*. The influence running both ways.
- [ ] Modeselektor — *Monkeytown* (2011). Berlin techno with Yorke singing on two tracks; the sound of *The King of Limbs* in the wild.
- [ ] Miles Davis — *Get Up with It* (1974). "He Loved Him Madly," a thirty-minute elegy for Ellington, is the record Eno called the root of ambient music and the one Yorke was chasing on *Kid A*. Also the deepest cut of the Miles catalog for someone who loves Miles and Duke both.

## 8. Jazz: the connections the Q&A opened

**What emerged:** you named five jazz loves. The list has their catalogs. These are the records that connect them to each other and to everything else on the list.

- [ ] ★ Joni Mitchell — *Mingus* (1979). Mingus, dying of ALS, asked Joni to write words for his last melodies. Jaco and Herbie play. A bridge from your Blue/Hejira path to your Mingus path.
- [ ] Hal Willner (producer) — *Weird Nightmare: Meditations on Mingus* (1992). Elvis Costello, Keith Richards, Chuck D, Henry Rollins and Dr. John, partly played on Harry Partch's instruments. Three of your threads in one record.
- [ ] Carlos Santana & John McLaughlin — *Love Devotion Surrender* (1973). A guitar tribute to Coltrane with "A Love Supreme" and "Naima."
- [ ] The Byrds — *Fifth Dimension* (1966). "Eight Miles High" is Coltrane's "India" played on a twelve-string Rickenbacker; the day jazz entered rock.
- [ ] Miles Davis — *A Tribute to Jack Johnson* (1971). Miles at his most rock, for a boxing documentary. John McLaughlin's guitar; the record the Stooges should have made.
- [ ] Miles Davis & Gil Evans — *Porgy and Bess* (1959). Between *Kind of Blue* and *Sketches of Spain*; Gershwin as a slow blue painting.
- [ ] Herbie Hancock — *Mwandishi* (1971) and *Sextant* (1973). The electric, spacey Herbie between Miles and *Head Hunters*; closer to Sun Ra than to funk.
- [ ] Horace Tapscott — *The Giant Is Awakened* (1969). Los Angeles's Sun Ra: a community orchestra leader whose records were almost never released.
- [ ] Gary Bartz NTU Troop — *Harlem Bush Music: Taifa* (1971). Spiritual jazz with sung poetry; a Coltrane disciple going political.
- [ ] Doug Carn — *Infant Eyes* (1971). Coltrane and Wayne Shorter tunes given lyrics and sung by Jean Carn; the Black Jazz label's masterpiece.
- [ ] Dorothy Ashby — *Afro-Harping* (1968). Jazz harp, funky, sampled by everyone; the other side of the Alice Coltrane coin.
- [ ] Note for the list: Teo Macero built *In a Silent Way* and *Bitches Brew* by cutting and splicing hours of tape into new shapes. It is Burroughs's cut-up method applied to jazz, and worth saying in the Miles path.

## 9. Hip-hop, deeper

**What emerged:** you said hip-hop belongs with the popular canon, and the list has the famous records. What it lacks is the literary, surreal, and concept-album wing, which is exactly your taste.

- [ ] ★ Deltron 3030 — *Deltron 3030* (2000). Del, Dan the Automator and Kid Koala make a dystopian space opera set in the year 3030; a rap *Sirens of Titan*.

- [ ] Dr. Octagon — *Dr. Octagonecologyst* (1996). Kool Keith as a time-traveling gynecologist from Jupiter. Surrealism that rap had never tried.
- [ ] Company Flow — *Funcrusher Plus* (1997). Dense, paranoid, independent as a rule; El-P's first statement.

- [ ] The Coup — *Steal This Album* (1998). Boots Riley's Marxist storytelling; Abbie Hoffman's title, on your book list, with a wink.
- [ ] Shabazz Palaces — *Black Up* (2011). Digable Planets' Butterfly, twenty years on, making alien, Sun Ra-adjacent rap.
- [ ] Digable Planets — *Blowout Comb* (1994). Jazz-rap as Black Panther Brooklyn, with live horns.
- [ ] Freestyle Fellowship — *Innercity Griots* (1993). LA rappers who came out of a jazz café's open mic; the Beats would recognize them.

- [ ] Edan — *Beauty and the Beat* (2005). Psychedelic 1960s rock cut into a rap record; thirty-five minutes.
- [ ] Death Grips — *The Money Store* (2012). The Pynchon of hip-hop: paranoid, loud, overloaded with information.

- [ ] Mobb Deep — *The Infamous* (1995). Queensbridge noir, the cold twin of *Illmatic*.
- [ ] Camp Lo — *Uptown Saturday Night* (1997). Blaxploitation slang over jazz loops; nobody understood a word and it didn't matter.
- [ ] Geto Boys — *We Can't Be Stopped* (1991). "Mind Playing Tricks on Me" is the paranoia of *Office Space*'s printer scene turned real.
- [ ] Ka — *Honor Killed the Samurai* (2016). A Brooklyn firefighter rapping quietly about honor over almost no beat.
- [ ] Roots Manuva — *Run Come Save Me* (2001). British rap's *Illmatic*, dubbed out and funny.
- [ ] Dave — *Psychodrama* (2019). A London rap album structured as a therapy session.

## 11. The year 1999, in records

## Proposed additions to the book list

What the music pass showed the book list is missing: the music books, especially the ones that are about how writing works.

- **But Beautiful** · Geoff Dyer. Jazz told as fiction: imagined scenes of Lester Young, Monk, Mingus, Chet Baker. The best-written book about music there is, and exactly the "how the style paints the picture" book your reading project wants.
- **Miles: The Autobiography** · Miles Davis with Quincy Troupe. Profane, funny, unreliable, essential.
- **Beneath the Underdog** · Charles Mingus. His memoir, written as a man talking to his psychiatrist; half true, all Mingus.
- **Space Is the Place: The Lives and Times of Sun Ra** · John Szwed. The biography of the man from Saturn.
- **This Planet Is Doomed** · Sun Ra. His science-fiction poems; belongs beside Ginsberg.
- **Psychotic Reactions and Carburetor Dung** · Lester Bangs. Rock criticism as gonzo literature; Hunter S. Thompson with a record collection.
- **The Old, Weird America** · Greil Marcus. The Basement Tapes and the Harry Smith *Anthology*; where Dylan's and your roots sections come from.
- **Lipstick Traces** · Greil Marcus. A secret history running from the Sex Pistols back through Dada and medieval heresy. A Robert Anton Wilson book in disguise.
- **Please Kill Me** · Legs McNeil & Gillian McCain. The oral history of punk; Burroughs and Patti Smith wander through.
- **Our Band Could Be Your Life** · Michael Azerrad. The American indie underground, 1981–91: Minutemen, Hüsker Dü, Replacements, Sonic Youth.
- **Rip It Up and Start Again** · Simon Reynolds. Post-punk 1978–84, the era half your list lives in.
- **Where Dead Voices Gather** · Nick Tosches. A search for a forgotten minstrel singer that becomes a meditation on American music and oblivion.
- **Do Not Sell at Any Price** · Amanda Petrusich. The obsessive world of 78-rpm collectors (Crumb among them); where Skip James and Charley Patton came back from.
- **Let's Talk About Love: Why Other People Have Bad Taste** · Carl Wilson. A book about hating Céline Dion that becomes a book about what taste is. For your Taylor Swift question.
- **Major Labels** · Kelefa Sanneh. A history of popular music in seven genres, each argued for by someone who loves it; a map of the territory your list covers.
- **A Little Devil in America** · Hanif Abdurraqib. Essays on Black performance, from Josephine Baker to Soul Train; a poet writing about music.
- **Musicophilia** · Oliver Sacks. Music and the brain; belongs on your consciousness shelf.
- **How Music Works** · David Byrne. Talking Heads' singer on the physics, economics and architecture of music.
- **Great Jones Street** · Don DeLillo. A rock star walks away mid-tour and holes up in a room; DeLillo's early novel, for the White Noise reader.
- **The Fortress of Solitude** · Jonathan Lethem. Brooklyn, 1970s, soul and early hip-hop, a magic ring; the great music novel of your era.

## Proposed additions to the movie list

Music films the movie list doesn't have, mostly documentaries, with the strongest ties to this list.

- **Pull My Daisy** (1959) · Robert Frank & Alfred Leslie. Kerouac narrates; Ginsberg and Corso play themselves. The Beat film.
- **Chappaqua** (1966) · Conrad Rooks. A drug-withdrawal fever dream with Burroughs, Ginsberg and an Ornette Coleman score.
- **Jazz on a Summer's Day** (1959) · Bert Stern. Newport 1958: Monk, Mahalia Jackson, Chuck Berry, Anita O'Day, in color.
- **Space Is the Place** (1974) · John Coney. Sun Ra lands his spaceship in Oakland. You said you love him; this is his movie.
- **Thelonious Monk: Straight, No Chaser** (1988) · Charlotte Zwerin. Monk in 1968, spinning in circles between tunes.
- **Let's Get Lost** (1988) · Bruce Weber. Chet Baker at the end, in black and white.
- **Thirty Two Short Films About Glenn Gould** (1993) · François Girard. Gould in 32 fragments; the *Slaughterhouse-Five* pianist, and a 90s-volume film.
- **Monterey Pop** (1968) · D.A. Pennebaker. Hendrix burns the guitar; Otis Redding; Ravi Shankar.
- **Wattstax** (1973) · Mel Stuart. The "Black Woodstock," with Isaac Hayes and Richard Pryor as narrator.
- **Summer of Soul** (2021) · Questlove. The 1969 Harlem Cultural Festival, found in a basement after fifty years: Nina Simone, Sly, Mahalia, Stevie at 19.
- **The Harder They Come** (1972) · Perry Henzell. The film that brought reggae to the world; its soundtrack is already on the music list.
- **Coal Miner's Daughter** (1980) · Michael Apted. Loretta Lynn's life; Sissy Spacek sings it herself. Jack White later produced Loretta's comeback.
- **Meeting People Is Easy** (1998) · Grant Gee. Radiohead on the *OK Computer* tour, falling apart; the film that explains *Kid A*.
- **Shut Up and Play the Hits** (2012) · Dylan Southern & Will Lovelace. LCD Soundsystem's farewell at Madison Square Garden and the morning after.
- **It Might Get Loud** (2008) · Davis Guggenheim. Jack White, Jimmy Page and the Edge in a room with guitars.
- **Under Great White Northern Lights** (2009) · Emmett Malloy. The White Stripes play every province of Canada, including a one-note show.
- **Big Time** (1988) · Chris Blum. Tom Waits's concert film from the *Franks Wild Years* tour, half stage show, half fever dream.
- **Coffee and Cigarettes** (2003) · Jim Jarmusch. Waits and Iggy Pop share a table; the Wu-Tang's RZA and GZA drink herbal tea with Bill Murray.
- **One from the Heart** (1982) · Francis Ford Coppola. The film Waits scored; a neon Las Vegas built on a soundstage.
- **Scott Walker: 30 Century Man** (2006) · Stephen Kijak. Bowie produced it; the only time Walker let a camera in.
- **The Devil and Daniel Johnston** (2005) · Jeff Feuerzeig. Mental illness, cassette tapes, and "Hi, How Are You."
- **Dig!** (2004) · Ondi Timoner. The Dandy Warhols and the Brian Jonestown Massacre, seven years of rivalry; the funniest music doc ever made.
- **The Decline of Western Civilization** (1981) · Penelope Spheeris. LA punk in 1980: X, Black Flag, the Germs.
- **High Fidelity** (2000) · Stephen Frears. The record-store film of your golden era; Jack Black's first real part.
- **The Rutles: All You Need Is Cash** (1978) · Eric Idle & Gary Weis. The Beatles parody with George Harrison in a cameo.
- **Get Back** (2021) · Peter Jackson. Eight hours of the Beatles in January 1969, if you know them all and want to see them work.
